The post-crash timeslice switch (Config::post_crash_timeslice).

A race that needs fine slices (--max-timeslice of 1000 to 10000) often reaches its crash in seconds and then spends minutes in the crash path: Android’s debuggerd handler starts crash_dump, which ptrace-attaches every thread of the crashed process, reads its registers and memory and unwinds it, and every branch of that is sliced. Once a guest process has crashed, the switch raises the slice bound of that process and of every process it goes on to create to the configured post-crash bound, for the rest of the run. Other processes (a harness that runs trials as children, a shell) keep their bound.

A crash is a fatal signal on its way to ending a process (is_crash_signal). Every step below happens at a point the schedule orders, so it is taken at the same point of every run and replay that reaches it:

  • a crash signal’s delivery that takes the default action switches at once, for the crashed process;
  • one the guest catches arms the switch for that thread. The processes that thread’s process creates while it is armed (debuggerd’s pseudothread, and crash_dump before its exec) belong to its crash;
  • a successful execve by a thread of such a process takes the switch for an armed crash of its own lineage whose handler is still running: Android’s crash handler starts crash_dump that way, from inside the handler. Crashes in unrelated processes keep separate lineages, and each crash in a process not yet switched takes a switch of its own. A switch applies to its whole process: every crash armed in it switches with it, and those crashes’ lineages take the bound too;
  • an armed thread’s handler has returned once the kernel no longer blocks its crash signal for it (signal_blocked). That is read only where the armed thread itself is stopped at a point of its own: each syscall entry, before the call runs; each syscall exit or trap, after it completes and before the thread can give up its turn there; each end of one of its slices; each later signal delivery to it, before the kernel installs that handler’s mask. A thread gives up its turn only at such a point, and with threads serialized no other thread runs in between, so the state known at another thread’s execve is the armed thread’s own. A handler that resumes the faulting code (ART’s and JVMs’ implicit null checks) is disarmed at its thread’s next stop, and never switches.

The scheduler keeps the PostCrashState; a thread of a crashed process learns the bound from the answer to its own step and from every later reply that resumes it from a grant (GlobalResponse::WithPostCrashBound), so it uses the bound from its first slice after the switch.

Functions§

admission_refusal
The refusal, by name, of a run whose --post-crash-timeslice switch is in effect on a backend that cannot see a caught crash’s handler return (reads_handler_masks): the switch would then take the default-action crashes and silently ignore every caught one, and the same guest would switch on ptrace and not there. Detcore has no handler-lifetime record of its own that a backend could share instead, so the run is refused before the guest starts (GlobalState::initialize), as unsupported options are. None when the switch is off or the backend reads the guests’ masks.
is_crash_signal
Whether signal, delivered with si_code (None when it could not be read), is a crash: SIGABRT however it was sent (abort sends it with tgkill), or SIGSEGV, SIGBUS, SIGILL, SIGFPE or SIGSYS raised by the kernel (si_code > 0; a kill, tgkill or sigqueue sends them with si_code <= 0, and a fault is the only kernel source of these).
